# Malton survival prototype

A standalone Godot 4 / GDScript survival game using the original project's artwork alongside new assets in the same pixel-art style. The historical Python source and builds remain untouched. Tested with Godot 4.7.2 and the Compatibility renderer.

## Run

Open `project.godot` in Godot and press F5 to run the project, or use PowerShell:

```powershell
.\launch.ps1
.\launch.ps1 -Editor
.\launch.ps1 -Test
.\launch.ps1 -Gallery
.\launch.ps1 -Balance shelter
.\launch.ps1 -Balance holdout
```

The launcher finds Godot on PATH or in a `Godot*` folder in Downloads. Override with `-GodotPath 'C:\path\to\godot.exe'`. Child processes use local `.runtime/` application data. Opening the project directly uses Godot's usual save location. Save displays the actual path; the game has one replaceable save slot.

The latest [rules audit](docs/urban-dead-rules-audit.md) distinguishes restored rules, remaining parity gaps and offline adaptations. [AI balance results](docs/ai-balance-results.md) records repeatable multi-day trials. Balance runs save per-day population, action and navigation metrics under `reports/`; the `shelter` suite covers ten scenarios and `holdout` uses additional seeds over fourteen days. The player is an inactive observer in these tests.

The minimap now assembles a connected aerial city with shared roads, three architectural states, six street variations, several mall designs, walled forts and continuous zoo grounds. See [connected minimap](docs/connected-minimap.md) and `tests/connected-minimap-gallery.png`. Zombies use a matching 24-frame atlas with slow staggering, claw strikes, recoil and a horizontal corpse; see `tests/zombie-animation-preview.png`.

## Playing

Normal launch opens `start_screen.tscn` with five scrolling city layers, a Malton Reborn menu and looping music. New Game opens the character creation room. Select an occupation and Confirm to play its introduction and start the original 100×100 Malton map. The illustrated intro advances by click or key, can be skipped, and respects the creation room's reduced-motion setting. Corpse starts always pause at Rise for the player's input before the awakening and fade. Back returns to the title. Load resumes the Godot save slot and reports missing or invalid saves. The slot updates when you save. F11 toggles fullscreen, and the title buttons support keyboard focus and activation.

The character room uses artwork in `assets/character_creation/`. Superseded Godot artwork and the menu's art prompts are in [the archive](../archive/godot/README.md), outside the Godot project.

Running `main.tscn` directly still starts the Volunteer at Anne General Hospital in Dulston for development. The Volunteer carries a bat, first aid kit, toolbox and syringe, knowing Construction, NecroTech Employment and Lab Experience. The old Mercy General opening remains available as the Legacy test neighbourhood. Restart opens New Game, with Original Malton, Random city and Legacy test neighbourhood modes. It offers the eight original survivor occupations, Corpse, and Volunteer. Random city exposes a seed, dimensions, exact ordinary building counts, numbers of mall/fort/zoo/cathedral/mansion/power-station/stadium complexes, and survivor/zombie populations. Invalid requests explain capacity or packing errors without replacing the running session. Each canonical occupation has its own portrait, equipment and starting skills. The Corpse's human portrait appears after revival.

Click nearby tiles or use arrows/WASD and Q/E/Z/C to move. Click a sprite to select a target. Right-click inventory items for contextual uses; click to select equipment or an item for Give/Drop. Space pauses; R rests for ten AP; M opens the neighbourhood map; Escape closes dialogs, cancels rest or pauses.

Only contextually supported actions appear. AP and recovery can temporarily disable them. Enter remains available at excessive barricades and explains the blocked entry without spending AP. Inventory uses percentage encumbrance, with ten items shown per page and Previous/Next controls for additional items. Collection stops at 100 items or 100% encumbrance; the last find can take load beyond 100%. Generators/transmitters weigh 20%, toolboxes 16%, fuel 10%, and small supplies usually 2%. Dropping discards an item permanently; there are no ground-item piles.

Humans and zombies retain identity, inventory and learned skills across death and revival. Downed players perceive no environment, map, NPC activity or history. Standing restores perception. Revival produces a downed human who must stand. The rules deliberately prevent permanent character death.

## World and presentation

- Original Malton imports 10,000 named squares across all 100 suburbs from an archived Wiki map. Both world modes use the original complex plans: twenty malls including Lumber's two squares and Pole's three-square L, both distinct 3×3 forts, the 5×4 zoo, and original cathedral, mansion, stadium and power-station footprints. Generated cities relocate these plans without overlapping occupied cells and repeat the plan sequence when more complexes are requested. Movement crosses suburb borders continuously. The map screen offers local 10×10 blocks, city suburb selection, direction browsing and Find me. There are 37 indoor and nine outdoor categories.
- Location text assembles three independently selected visual sentences from concise pools, then adds context-sensitive doors, barricades, lighting, visible interior damage and occupants. Text persists in saves, wraps below the scene and fits without scrolling. Closed doors are mentioned only without barricades. Outside observers see lights rather than generator details; structural damage is visible even behind closed doors. Barricade attack sounds belong to the activity feed.
- Minimap and full map share the connected aerial renderer. Roads and curbs meet across touching tiles; compound interiors use continuous paths instead of roads. Every building has unlit, lit and ruined art. Large buildings share one model, cropped in world coordinates, with lighting and damage selected independently per square. Four standard mall designs and a dedicated L-shaped mall add variety. Fort walls remain continuous around ruined sectors and open at the original gatehouse position. Offices and NecroTech sites share the same map architecture; Employment reveals an NT logo. Warm windows convey power, damaged architecture conveys ruin, and a gold outline marks the player. Names are available in movement tooltips. Discovery and timestamped scans replace omniscient population information.
- The environment displays its full image height without cropping, with a 1016×572 view. Sprite frames are aligned by their opaque foot bounds and grounded with subtle shadows. Rest controls sit below the portrait, inventory follows beneath, and contextual actions use compact rows directly below the environment.
- Larger selectable sprites face each other: humans left, zombies right. Crowds overlap and stagger in depth. Red HP bars are short and thick; NPC AP remains hidden. Diagnosis and Scent Blood expose relevant health details in tooltips.
- Nine distinct full-body human/revived occupation atlases replace shared generic art. Each 6×4 atlas retains 24 frames and uses all four sequences: idle (0–7), attack (8–14), death (15–20), hurt (21–23). Zombie gait uses slower irregular holds. Combat reactions survive interface rebuilds.
- Nine new 5×5 portrait atlases provide healthy breathing, two injury states, static death and irregular undead lurching. Cosmetic animation randomness does not consume simulation RNG.
- NPC speech appears in bubbles and the local activity feed, with occupation and situation-specific human dialogue and undead noises. Cooldowns keep chatter occasional; fast rest and unconsciousness suppress it.

## Skills and combat

The skill menu groups human skills into Military, Scientific, and Civilian tracks, and zombie skills into Scent, Offensive & Attack, and Utility & Brain tracks. Headshot requires level ten and appears under Military. The machine-readable catalogue is in `assets/generated/skill-catalogue.json`.

Military/science occupations pay 75 XP within their category, 150 for the opposite category and 100 for civilian/Hunter skills. Civilian occupations pay 100 for human skills. Zombie skills always cost 100. Both trees can be purchased in either form as in the earlier prototype. Skills normally activate in their matching form; Body Building and NecroTech recognition survive death, while Ankle Grab and Scent Death remain useful after revival.

Zombie claws deal two damage at 25% base accuracy, 35% with Vigour Mortis, and 50% with Death Grip. Neck Lurch unlocks a four-damage bite at 30%. Rend Flesh raises it to five damage at 40%. Infectious Bite makes successful bites infect survivors for one HP per AP spent until first aid. Tangling Grasp reduces the held survivor’s attack accuracy by ten percentage points. A miss has a 50% chance to release the grip, and movement, death, and revival release it. Zombies can still wield crowbars, baseball bats, and pipes.

Human combat applies firearms, pistol/shotgun, hand-to-hand, knife and axe training. Fists hit at 10% or 25% with Hand-to-Hand Combat. Flare guns hit at 2.5% or 15% with Basic Firearms Training, for 15 damage as a single-use shot; fractional accuracy is preserved. Hockey sticks deal three damage. Pistols and shotguns start at 15% and reach 65% with full training. Trained knives reach 50%. Axe Proficiency raises firefighters to the 40% axe cap. Pistols take a six-round magazine; shotguns load one shell per action, hold two, and deal ten damage. Flare guns are disposable shots or signals. Flak jackets and Flesh Rot reduce firearm damage by 20% without stacking. Melee weapons do not wear out. A pool cue has a 20% chance to splinter on each successful hit and is removed from inventory when it breaks; misses never break it. Bottles still break on successful hits.

The minimap shows up to three visible survivor names per tile, an ellipsis for overflow, and an anonymous zombie count. Scent Fear marks survivors below 25 HP as wounded and below 13 HP as dying. Diagnosis reads exact HP while alive. Scent Blood reads exact HP and highlights infection while undead. Scent Trail automatically follows the latest survivor to interact with the zombie until they reach 10 blocks apart or either character dies. Revival also clears a zombie’s trail. Zombie NPCs need Scent Trail to pursue survivors. Scent Death identifies revivifying bodies in both forms. The zombie action costs 1 AP and opens a five-block radius heat map with red for bodies, green for standing zombies, and mixed colours for both. Death Lurch reduces standing to 5 AP and movement to 1 AP. Lurching Gait also reduces movement to 1 AP, and Ankle Grab takes standing to 1 AP before any Headshot penalty. Lab Experience enables 1 AP revivals, or 10 AP for Brain Rot with powered lab access. Radio Operation gates installing, tuning, and broadcasting on a transmitter.

Powered NecroTech labs offer NecroNet Access and 20 AP syringe manufacture to trained survivors. The 9 by 9 scan uses block-type colours and shows active DNA-tag counts, with a saved snapshot available from the field map.

Offices, NecroTech buildings, hotels and towers provide window jumps for either form. A survivor with binoculars can choose a compass direction indoors at these buildings to survey a 3 by 3 area. From a street, square or monument, binoculars open a centred 7 by 7 survey and identify skyline landmarks within ten blocks. A survivor dies and can pay the usual standing AP to rise as a zombie. A zombie lands unharmed on the same block. Some originally undead NPCs prefer that form and seek an accessible tall building after revival.

Other skill effects include targeted mall stores and Bargain Hunting, 60-HP Body Building/Flesh Rot, tagging, restricted radio channels, building repair, free running, safehouse scouting, DNA extraction/tags, NecroNet and syringe manufacture, First Aid/Surgery/Diagnosis, Headshot standing penalties, scents, corpse feeding, infection, dragging, zombie speech, groans/bellowing, ransacking, gestures, movement and standing discounts, and Brain Rot revival restrictions.

## Shelters, loot and NPCs

Everyone has a 50-AP cap and recovers one AP per thirty game minutes. At normal speed one real second equals one game minute. Actions take effect immediately, then impose individual recovery; there is no global NPC turn. Rest runs at 16× and downed recovery at 32× until standing becomes possible. Pause and dialogs freeze time. Rest stops on injury, danger or state changes.

Barricades have 21 strength points: loose 1, light 2–4, quite strong 5–7, strong 8, very strong 9–10, heavy 11–13, very heavy 14–16 and extremely heavy 17–21. Attacks roll character-versus-player accuracy, then a separate 50% structural chance; crowbars bypass that second roll. Human melee skills apply, giving a trained crowbar and fully trained axe a 20% total damage chance. Human fists start at 10% before skills and the structural roll. Guns, knives, fuel cans and zombie bites are ineffective. Messages distinguish misses, harmless creaks and actual damage. Each damaging hit removes one point; successful construction restores one point. Survivors can enter from outside through strength 10, with a warning on reaching that limit; stronger barricades require Free Running from an adjacent building. The fourteen original open zoo squares cannot be barricaded; all six enclosures can be barricaded but have no closable doors. Fort entry is gatehouse-only. Map-click exits from any perimeter square lead outside, including at EHB. Free Running out is allowed only from inside the gatehouse at VSB or lower, and internal Free Running cannot enter the Armoury. Human and zombie mall interiors connect without Free Running. Junkyards have a fence opening rather than doors; churches, cathedrals, fort gatehouses and zoo enclosures also lack closable doors. Zombies need Memories of Life to enter through closed doors and leave those doors open on entry. Main-door exits work for either form through VSB. A human preserves the door’s previous state when passing through, while a zombie secures the doors on exit. At heavier barricade levels, an adjacent-block move provides a ground exit.

Barricade construction uses the current strength band: 100% at zero, 99.5% loose, 99% light, 97.8% quite strong, 85.1% strong and very strong, 61.5% heavy, 38% very heavy and 20.5% extremely heavy. Successful construction closes functional doors automatically and adds one strength point for one AP, without a separate Close action or a required carried tool. A survivor without Construction can create one loose barricade indoors by using a length of pipe. A successful build consumes the pipe. Construction is required to reinforce an existing barricade, build without a pipe, or repair ruins. Two or more living, standing zombies inside halve the odds; unpowered dark buildings (banks, cinemas, nightclubs, armouries and malls) halve them again. A single intruder does not interfere, and intruders no longer prohibit construction. At strength 21, attempts report "The barricade cannot be built any higher." and spend no AP, including when the player has zero AP. Successful builds, failures, interference and the strength-ten entry warning have distinct feedback.

Installed generators prevent ransacking; eligible ruined buildings accept generators. Generators have six HP, descriptive damage, and must be fully repaired before refueling. Construction and a toolbox are required for structural ruin and generator repairs; ordinary ransack cleanup costs 1 AP without them. A fuel can tops fuel up to 24 hours and wastes the excess. Dark ruins require power to repair. Fuel cans, generators and other supplies use plausible site-specific loot tables.

The catalogue has 54 item types. Searches first roll for any find using building state, then select an item from normalized site/store loot weights. Lit buildings find items at 30% (NecroTech 28%, malls 26%); intact unlit buildings use 20%, inherently dark unpowered sites use 1%, otherwise ransacked/ruined buildings use 10%, and outside searches use 5% anywhere with one shared street grab bag of junk, tools and survival gear. Outdoor loot excludes syringes, firearms and ammunition. Shopping targets mall stores and Bargain Hunting doubles intact indoor mall yields, producing 52% total finds in a lit mall. Firearms are partially loaded 75% of the time and otherwise empty. The [search rules](docs/search-rules.md) document exact choices and the distinction between total find rates and conditional item weights. Player searches describe discarded junk and usable finds; NPC narration remains concise.

The legacy neighbourhood begins with 15 of 18 zombies dormant, including indoor bodies. Malton and generated cities offer Easy, Medium and Hard population presets with about 84% dormant at the start. Medium is the default. Generated maps scale the presets by area, and Custom accepts exact survivor and zombie counts. See the [population trials](docs/ai-balance-results.md#population-presets--5-november-2026) for counts and outcomes. Each dormant body has a 20% chance of becoming eligible to rise at midnight. Dump bodies outside before they reanimate inside. Survivor groups start with two or three members, maintain shelters, heal and defend allies, plan supply trips and install/refuel equipment. Assistance builds trust; strangers refuse supply demands and trusted NPCs protect essentials. NPCs use the same weapon stats, skill bonuses, fractional hit rolls and damage rules as the player. Human weapon choice compares expected damage against the selected target, including armour, remaining HP, ammunition availability and reload AP; equal scores preserve current equipment. Zombie attack choice compares claw and bite damage, adding usable Digestion healing and one prospective infection-damage point for an uninfected survivor who would survive the bite. Zombies pursue prey, investigate light, breach and ransack. NPCs reserve return AP, keep constructors at shared homes, run lab revival queues and conserve generator fuel. Two thirds of former survivor NPCs seek revival after death. NPCs start with the same skills as their corresponding player occupations; Corpse and Resident NPCs begin with Vigour Mortis and must learn Memories of Life and Ransack. Work parties may carry additional supplies. Multi-seed balance reports are in `reports/`; long-term balance still needs playtesting.

Reading consumes a book or poetry book and reveals a random passage. Books have a 10% chance to grant 1 XP, or 2 XP for a scientific occupation. Poetry grants no XP. Survivors can equip newspapers and swat either form for 1 AP at 100% base accuracy with zero damage. Drinks, candy, clothing and decorations have contextual uses. Radios can be installed, repaired, tuned and broadcast over local channels; mobile phones require two handsets and powered masts covering both characters’ current suburbs. All 100 original mast sites are imported; generated districts receive one where they contain a suitable building. Binoculars reveal exterior figures from tall buildings; GPS/maps provide location information. Seasonal decorations are available as sandbox loot rather than calendar-gated events.

## Architecture and validation

- `scripts/catalog.gd`: item/skill catalogues, occupation mappings and tuning.
- `scripts/simulation.gd`: world, transactions, time, AI and saves, independent of UI.
- `scripts/extended_rules.gd`: item, communication and extended skill actions.
- `scripts/interface.gd`: presentation, animations and contextual controls.
- `scripts/event_feed.gd` and `dialogue.gd`: bounded feedback and NPC speech.
- `scripts/npc_ai.gd`: fort-aware paths, shelters, expeditions, combat choices and revival queues.

`launch.ps1 -Test` imports assets, runs simulation, survival, loot/trust and content-expansion checks, then rendered UI and art-gallery checks. Validation includes full-city and rendered world-setup checks, plus dedicated barricade accuracy, flavour, structural-strength and entry-boundary tests. UI tests require a graphics-capable desktop. Test saves use separate filenames. Tests cover lifecycle, deterministic save/replay, multi-seed population invariants, shelter balance, search/trust, skill purchases, combat/grasp, equipment/communications, atlas health/animation states, crowds and description bounds. They do not establish long-term gameplay balance.

The prototype is a local single-player city simulation: no server messaging, campaigns, gameplay sound effects or remappable controls. The title track plays through character creation and quiets during the intro. Gameplay shuffles imported MP3, Ogg Vorbis and WAV tracks in `assets/music/` at 30% volume. `summoning.mp3` is reserved for the title. The playlist restarts with a fresh shuffle after a successful New Game, Restart or Load. Original geography is available offline. New saves include map dimensions, mode, generator settings, provenance, names and complex identities; legacy 10×10 saves still load. Legacy pickle saves are not imported. Clothing is represented by one generic item rather than every named garment; decorative colour/name variants share representative assets. See the audit for additional differences from the original online game.

Encumbrance values, sources, zero-weight/unique items, local custom-item assignments, door/barricade migration and layout checks are documented in `docs/encumbrance-and-layout.md`.

Fort entry, one-way perimeter exits, gatehouse Free Running, five-AP body dumping and ruined-roof falls are detailed in [fort and Free Running rules](docs/fort-and-free-running.md).

## Widescreen, Sleep and revised environments

The interface targets 1920 x 1080, with a scaled 1536 x 864 window default and F11/full-screen control. Dialogue fills the right column; description and action controls sit below the scene. Sleep replaces Rest to 50 AP, runs at 128x speed, hides perception and wakes at full AP, a collapsing barricade or sustained injury. Wake/Escape also ends it.

All 54 items and 46 location types have audited search coverage, including the previously unreachable map and dead fir tree. See [search rules](docs/search-rules.md) for exact prototype rates and the distinction between sourced locations and estimated weights. [Environment and ruin rules](docs/environment-and-ruin-rules.md) documents NecroTech descriptions, true powered-ruin artwork, generator eligibility, damage progression, daily repair decay and AP debt.
