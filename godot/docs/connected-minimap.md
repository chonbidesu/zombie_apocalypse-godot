# Connected aerial city minimap

The 3×3 minimap and discovered 10×10 field map render one layered city scene. `scripts/minimap_canvas.gd` draws all terrain, then southeast cast shadows, then buildings and raised wall segments in depth order. Only the map viewport clips the artwork; gameplay squares remain invisible hit regions. Buildings fill their plots, project slightly north over the street and can overlap adjacent squares. A one-square halo includes roofs and shadows cast by neighbors outside the viewport. Roads have a narrower shared asphalt surface and continuous curbs. A street square contains one of six stable arrangements of demolished buildings around an open road. Variants derive from coordinates and never consume simulation randomness or change when the player moves or reloads.

`scripts/minimap_art.gd` selects and caches art. Six building atlases contain three columns (unlit, lit, ruined) and six rows each. NecroTech uses the office model to preserve concealment. `malls.png` supplies three more mall styles; `mall-l.png` supplies the three-square inner-corner architecture. The other ordinary building types each use one consistent architectural style. `outdoors.png` provides eight outdoor plot types; `streets-v2.png` supplies six genuinely transparent street arrangements, replacing a milky partial-alpha background in the first street atlas. `materials.png` provides asphalt, paving, grass and compound ground textures. Source PNGs remain intact. Godot trims unused space against each model's unlit bounds and selects AtlasTexture regions. Exact built-in generation prompts and paths are in `assets/generated/connected-minimap-manifest.json` and `assets/generated/minimap-depth-manifest.json`.

`scripts/minimap_ground.gd` determines joins using neighboring complex/fort identities. Roads disappear across internal compound edges. `fort-properties-v4.png` composes each original fort as one connected property, with its own architecture, yards, shared surfaces, shadows and three states. The renderer adds raised masonry wall faces, caps, supports, wire and ground shadows. The north wall rises behind the buildings and projects into the street beyond; the south wall draws in front of their bases. Creedy has cooler walls and an east-facing gatehouse seen in profile: its front tower overlaps the rear tower and hides the passage. The north return draws behind the gatehouse and the south return in front. Perryn retains warmer masonry and its visible south-facing entrance. Perimeter openings and short bonded returns meet the gatehouse doorway jambs. The opening follows the doorway's ground projection, not its rooftop center. Only the gatehouse offers entry, and the existing rule requiring entry into the gatehouse before entering the grounds remains in force.

`zoo-property-v2.png` paints the original 5×4 zoo as one continuous landscape with winding paths, irregular tree groves, planting, ponds and six distinct enclosure sites. Each grounds square samples a different part of that landscape, eliminating repeated tree pods and cross-shaped paths. Three states allow independent enclosure lighting and ruin. Original fort/zoo component layouts are checked before selecting property artwork. Older saves with different compound plans retain individual models and their stored geography.

Malls, cathedrals, mansions, stadiums and power stations sample one architectural model across their full footprint. Each square selects its own state from its own generator, fuel and ruin data: full ruin (`ruin >= 3`) overrides lighting. `minimap_state.gdshader` retains the common structural edge along joined sections while displaying local lighting or roof damage. One section changing state does not change its neighbors. Partial ransacking keeps the existing intact presentation until full ruin. Pole Mall uses a dedicated L-shaped model with an inner facade, and the absent fourth square remains an independent city block.

`assets/maps/complex-plans.json` derives from the archived coordinate data in `malton.json`, through `tools/import_malton.py:complex_plans`. Random generation relocates these original occupied-cell plans, preserving component positions and missing corners. Twenty malls use 77 squares, both forts use nine each, the zoo uses twenty, and the two power stations use two and four squares. Additional requested complexes repeat their category's plan sequence with unique names. Capacity accounting uses occupied cells rather than rectangular bounds; missing cells may contain unrelated plots. Existing saves keep their stored geography; the renderer handles their older rectangular compounds too.

Validation: `tests/connected_minimap_test.gd` checks building states, street alpha, variants, atlas joins, independent power/ruin, shared compound artwork, gatehouse perimeter sides, roof overlap, map-only clipping, modal depth, simulation RNG isolation and original footprints. It saves `tests/connected-minimap-game.png`, `tests/connected-minimap-gallery.png`, `tests/minimap-depth-preview.png` and `tests/fort-gatehouse-preview.png`. The two detailed previews use independent SubViewports so the desktop window cannot crop them. `launch.ps1 -Test` includes this check.

The profile correction and exact built-in edit prompt are recorded in `assets/generated/fort-gatehouse-profile-manifest.json`. Movement rules and the user-selected one-way wall exits are documented in [fort and Free Running rules](fort-and-free-running.md).
